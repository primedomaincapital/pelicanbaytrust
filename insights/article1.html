Why Florida's Dynasty Trusts Are Reshaping Estate Planning

The 1,000-year rule changed the math for multigenerational wealth — and the fiduciaries of Pelican Bay are at the center of it.
Prime Domain Capital · October 2026 · 6 min read

In 2022, Florida quietly did something no other large state had done: it extended the permissible life of a trust to 1,000 years. The rule against perpetuities — the centuries-old doctrine that forced trusts to dissolve within roughly a century — was effectively retired. A family that funds a Florida dynasty trust today can shelter appreciating assets from estate tax for forty generations.

The estate-planning bar noticed immediately. Within two years, Florida had joined South Dakota, Nevada, and Delaware in the top tier of trust jurisdictions, and trust companies across the state reported a marked increase in inbound inquiries from families in high-tax states looking to decant existing trusts into Florida vehicles.

What a 1,000-year trust actually does

The mechanics are straightforward and powerful. Assets placed in a properly structured Florida dynasty trust grow free of estate, gift, and generation-skipping transfer tax at each generational handoff — not once, but indefinitely. A $20 million portfolio compounding at 6 percent becomes roughly $360 million over a century inside the trust wrapper, versus a fraction of that if estate tax bites at every transfer.

Florida adds further advantages: no state income tax on trust earnings, strong asset-protection statutes, and since 2023, directed-trust legislation that lets families separate investment management from distribution decisions — useful when the trustee is chosen for judgment rather than portfolio skill.

“A thousand-year trust is not a tax strategy. It is a statement that the family intends to be a family for a very long time.”

Why Pelican Bay, specifically

The families most likely to fund dynasty trusts are not hypothetical. They live in Pelican Bay, in Port Royal, in Grey Oaks — second- and third-generation wealth holders for whom estate planning is a standing agenda item, not a one-time event. The trustee they choose will administer the vehicle for decades. Proximity matters. Reputation matters more.

This is where a place-based trust brand carries structural advantage. A family selecting a trustee for a thousand-year instrument is not shopping on price. They are shopping on permanence — and nothing signals permanence like a name rooted in the community the family calls home.

The counterweight

Dynasty trusts are not without critics. Some scholars argue that thousand-year instruments concentrate wealth in ways the rule against perpetuities was designed to prevent, and a future legislature could revisit the statute. Prudent planners build flexibility into trust instruments — decanting provisions, trust protectors, adaptable distribution standards — so the vehicle can evolve if the law does.

Even so, the direction of travel is clear. The families of Pelican Bay are planning on a generational timescale, and Florida has given them the longest runway in the country. The fiduciaries who serve them will need names equal to the horizon.
